# Prop library proposal — awaiting user review

This is an art/interaction plan, not delivered assets, a complete historical catalog, or authorization to start implementation. The user requested an era review first. No prop pixels, pickup, equipment, opening or breakage behavior were added during the historical-framework pass.

## Design rule

Separate **behavior**, **physical definition**, **visual recipe**, and **historical selection**. A plain earthen jar can share one drawing and container behavior between an appropriate Egyptian and Indian context. A jar of water and a jar of grain need different contents, not two culture-specific implementations. Capacity, contents and carried weight determine whether a particular instance is portable. Do not make a giant storage jar pick-uppable merely because a small jar is.

Choose variations that change the readable silhouette: wide mouth versus narrow neck, handled versus unhandled, rounded versus rectangular, low versus raised, open versus lidded. Material ramps, lids, handles, binding and decoration can reuse parts, but color swaps alone are insufficient. Existing building/shadow contracts should be reused for foot anchors and cast silhouettes.

The shared library is an authoring budget. Every era/family selection below is a **candidate for a local profile**, not a blanket statement that it belongs everywhere in that range. Dating and context remain necessary, especially for ritual/elite objects and imported goods.

## Era breakdown

Later periods retain appropriate older objects. Entries are additions/directions to investigate, not universal unlock dates. Finer introductions and distribution belong in local dated rules.

| Fixed era | Portable/storage priorities | Agriculture, water and daily-work priorities |

## Initial reference anchors and limits

These establish directions for research, not an authenticated complete matrix. All unsourced allocations above remain proposals. Museum survival/collection biases matter: a royal, funerary or ceremonial object must not automatically become ordinary household furniture.

- [Çatalhöyük Research Project](https://www.catalhoyuk.com/node/56): settlement context for the first Neolithic art family; object forms and mechanics still need specific references.
- [Smithsonian: early pottery](https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/behavior/carrying-storing/oldest-pottery): a reason to use regional dates instead of globally locking all pottery behind agriculture.
- [Met: Roman transport amphorae relief](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/251843): sealed vessels and commercial handling; not a generic household-loot distribution.
- [Met: Egyptian storage jar, c. 1328–1318 BCE](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/549580): tall sealed jar silhouette. Its embalming-cache context does not establish everyday contents or prevalence.
- [British Museum: Tang storage jar](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/A_1946-0715-1): a dated lidded/glazed form. The described burial association is a reason not to distribute this elaborate version indiscriminately.
- [Met: Indian water vase/lota](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/453159): investigate a distinct metal-vessel silhouette. The elaborate example is not proof of an ordinary medieval household's possessions.
- [Canadian Museum of History: Haida bentwood boxes](https://www.historymuseum.ca/teachers-zone/haida-arts-and-technologies/bentwood-boxes/): a construction tradition worth distinguishing from a European hinged chest; local dates and use matter.

Before drawing a scene's new prop set, assemble a small reference board from archaeological reports, dated museum objects and everyday-life evidence. Label replicas/reconstructions as such. No downloaded reference imagery has been incorporated into our assets in this phase.

## Interaction MVP after approval

1. Give a small portable container persistent identity, physical definition, visual recipe, contents, ownership, open/broken state and a ground/carried location. Ground props and carried instances must refer to the same object, not duplicate inventories.
2. **Space with empty hands:** pick up an adjacent eligible object; prefer the facing direction, otherwise a deterministic nearest target. If it cannot be carried, explain why and offer inspection/opening separately. One press is one action; suppress repeats while held and ignore gameplay keys while typing or in a modal.
3. **Space with an equipped usable object:** use its capability against the nearby facing target. A stick can perform a blunt strike; whether a vessel breaks depends on the target's material/resistance. No culture checks in the action handler.
4. Keep inspect/open/transfer reachable even while holding a tool. Opening is not synonymous with breaking. Keep a Wait control after Space changes purpose. Include an explicit way to put down/place a carried container and clear the hands.
5. Pick up, put down, transfer and break must preserve identity and quantities. Breaking spills existing contents once and persists the damaged state; no respawning treasure. Closed/lidded/sealed/locked are distinct concepts, but start with open/closed and defer locks/seals until a selected scene needs them.
6. Preserve ownership on pickup and dropping; theft/consequences should use existing shared rules. Choose simple carrying limits, not physics simulation. Avoid arbitrary-depth container nesting in the MVP.
7. Version the save/content schema deliberately. Test pickup → carry → put down → open/transfer → strike/break → save/reload/replay in both initial scenes, with conservation and input-focus checks.

This is the next proposed implementation slice. No new settlement generation, occupation economy, tool physics, or full inventory redesign is bundled into it. Review the twelve-era framework and this art scope with the user before starting.
